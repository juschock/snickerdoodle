## 1. Executive strategy

Snickerdoodle should not try to build a large social following before it has a reliable sales motion. The first growth engine should be:

```text
Visible campaign trigger
        ↓
Personal, useful outreach
        ↓
Matching Snickerdoodle sample
        ↓
Campaign survey
        ↓
Customer commitment while Gate B is closed
        ↓
Future reviewed $99 payment flow (only after Gate B)
        ↓
Human-reviewed delivery
        ↓
Permissioned proof + referral
```

The marketing thesis is:

> Find small organizations that already have a real campaign deadline, show them the closest finished example, and make the $99 decision easier than assembling the campaign themselves.

The first 90 days are not a contest for reach. They are a disciplined search for:

1. the segment that recognizes the value fastest;
2. the trigger that creates urgency without manufactured scarcity;
3. the sample that causes prospects to start the survey;
4. the language customers use when describing the problem;
5. the repeat/referral mechanism that lowers acquisition work over time.

### What wins at $0

- specificity over reach;
- visible proof over promises;
- a timely trigger over demographic targeting;
- an explicitly assigned operator's personal credibility over a faceless brand account;
- direct links to a relevant sample over a generic homepage link;
- permissioned customer outcomes over manufactured testimonials;
- consistent follow-up over posting volume.

### What this plan will not use

- paid advertising or boosted posts;
- purchased, scraped, or rented email lists;
- mass cold email automation;
- influencer payments, referral fees, or giveaways;
- introductory discounts that weaken the already-low $99 price;
- a daily content treadmill;
- new SaaS subscriptions;
- fake urgency, invented scarcity, or guaranteed results;
- unpermissioned customer names, logos, work, or results.

### Core promise

> One survey. A complete campaign package.

### Expanded value proposition

> Snickerdoodle turns one campaign goal into a coordinated, human-reviewed set of launch-ready words and a practical posting schedule—without an agency retainer or another software dashboard.

## 4. Ideal customer profile and sequencing

### Primary beachhead: small, local nonprofits and community organizations

Start here for the first 30–45 days.

**Best-fit triggers**

- a fundraiser, adoption event, volunteer drive, registration deadline, year-end appeal, sponsor ask, or community event scheduled in the next 2–8 weeks;
- a small team or volunteer organizer visibly managing several channels;
- an existing website and public facts, but scattered or incomplete campaign messaging;
- one concrete desired action such as donate, attend, volunteer, register, or sponsor.

**Why first**

- the need is time-bound and publicly visible;
- Snickerdoodle already has two closely matched public samples;
- the fixed price is accessible relative to agency work;
- organizers often know peer organizations, creating a natural referral network.

### Secondary segment: local restaurants and event-driven businesses

Begin testing after the nonprofit motion has at least 30 qualified outreach attempts.

**Best-fit triggers**

- opening, relaunch, seasonal menu, catering push, themed night, tasting, community event, or new signature offer;
- inconsistent messaging across website, Google Business Profile, email, and social;
- one action such as visit, reserve, order, call, redeem, or review.

Use the **Restaurant Local Discovery** sample.

### Third segment: appointment businesses with a seasonal opening

Test only after the first two segments produce data.

Examples include pet grooming, salons, tutors, photographers, and seasonal service businesses. Qualify around a specific booking window rather than selling ongoing marketing.

### Good-fit qualification

A prospect is a strong fit when all are true:

1. there is one real campaign within 60 days;
2. the audience is identifiable;
3. the desired action is identifiable;
4. the organization can provide accurate facts;
5. the buyer wants campaign materials, not outsourced posting or design;
6. $99 can be approved without a long procurement process.

### Disqualifiers

- a full rebrand or website rebuild;
- ongoing social media management;
- custom graphic production;
- crisis communication or regulated claims;
- political persuasion work;
- a request to guarantee campaign performance;
- several unrelated audiences or goals in one package;
- a deadline the review team cannot safely meet.

## 5. Zero-cost funnel

### Stage 1: discovery

Sources, in priority order:

1. personal and professional network;
2. public local-event calendars and organization websites;
3. Google Maps and Google Search;
4. LinkedIn people and organization pages;
5. Facebook public events and community groups where promotion is permitted;
6. partner introductions from printers, designers, photographers, event venues, bookkeepers, and web developers;
7. organic search through existing sample pages.

Do not scrape. Research a small number of real prospects manually and record the public trigger that makes contact relevant.

### Stage 2: proof

Send the prospect to the closest sample—not the homepage by default.

| Prospect trigger | Proof link |
| --- | --- |
| Adoption or rescue event | `/snickerdoodle/samples/adoption-event` |
| Fundraising or donor deadline | `/snickerdoodle/samples/year-end-appeal` |
| Restaurant or local discovery | `/snickerdoodle/samples/restaurant-local-discovery` |
| Other good fit | `/snickerdoodle/samples` |

The sample's job is to create this reaction:

> “This would save me time and make the campaign feel coordinated.”

### Stage 3: fit check

The default fit check is asynchronous and should take under 10 minutes of assigned-operator time.

Ask:

1. What is the campaign?
2. When does it launch or end?
3. Who needs to act?
4. What is the one most important action?
5. Are the dates, prices, links, and claims final?

If it fits, generate an expiring, email-bound private survey link. A separately frozen, three-review-approved individual handoff may send that exact link after the qualified fit check. Never reuse the link for another address, record it in a public tracker, or publish a direct survey CTA while Gate B is closed. Do not force a sales call for a $99 purchase. Offer a 15-minute call only when ambiguity blocks the decision.

### Stage 4: commitment, then conversion after Gate B

While Gate B is closed, the fit check and survey should repeat the scope without implying that payment is available. After Gate B opens, the survey and future payment flow should repeat:

- exact deliverables;
- fixed price;
- timing start condition;
- human review;
- exclusions;
- privacy and payment safety.

### Stage 5: advocacy

Every successful delivery gets three follow-ups:

1. **48 hours after delivery:** “Were you able to use the package?”
2. **After the campaign's key date:** ask what shipped and what was useful.
3. **If satisfied:** request permission for a concise testimonial and one introduction.

Never publish work or results without explicit written permission.

### Website conversion priorities

The local release-candidate website contains the fixed price, deliverable list, 48-hour expectation, examples, exclusions, FAQ, and three complete samples. Its public conversion CTAs are **view a sample** and **request a fit check**; `/brief` is unlisted, omitted from the sitemap, and `noindex`. Verify that exact behavior again on Preview and production before Gate A opens.

Use conversation and funnel data to make only evidence-backed changes:

1. Add the $99 price nearer the first screen only if prospects repeatedly assume agency pricing.
2. Change the hero's secondary CTA from “See What's Included” to “View a Full Sample” if sample links outperform section navigation.
3. Add a “How did you hear about us?” field to intake once attribution volume justifies a code change.
4. Add one segment-specific landing page only after that segment produces paid orders.
5. Add a customer story only after written permission; never replace the fictional-sample disclosure with ambiguous wording.

Every outreach link should land on the closest proof page and use a consistent source tag where practical.

## 7. Content system

### Media decision rule

Before creating an image, ask: “Would the post lose essential meaning as text?” If no, publish text-only. The operating target is **at least 90% text-only posts** and **no more than one AI-image candidate per month** during the first 90 days. Creating and reviewing an image candidate consumes that week's social-action preparation slot and cancels the text social action; an approved image publication never adds volume.

When an AI image is justified:

1. prefer abstract/editorial still-life concepts without recognizable people, minors, public figures, logos, flags, political or religious symbols, disasters, medical scenes, or embedded text;
2. freeze the exact image, caption, alt text, platform, and destination URL;
3. calculate a SHA-256 file hash;
4. submit the full-resolution asset independently to three distinct, top-level ChatGPT sessions that did not generate it;
5. require all three to complete the full error, scandal, bias, privacy, intellectual-property, misleading-claim, and hostile-cropping checklist;
6. treat any concern or uncertainty as a `BLOCK`, not a majority vote;
7. regenerate blocked images and restart all three reviews for the new version;
8. obtain explicit owner approval for the exact version after all passes;
9. wait 24 hours, run the final publication preflight, and post manually;
10. inspect the live post immediately and again within 24 hours.

The generating session does not count as a reviewer. Cropping, resizing, upscaling, recoloring, text overlay, or a meaning-changing caption creates a new version and restarts review. Full instructions and the reviewer prompt are in `docs/marketing/image-publication-gate.md`.

### Four content pillars

1. **Campaign clarity** — one audience, one action, one sequence.
2. **Campaign anatomy** — explain what a complete package contains.
3. **Proof** — show small excerpts and structure from fictional or permissioned campaigns.

**Diagnose**

> “If your campaign calendar says only ‘post on Facebook,’ the missing piece is the job each post must do.”

## 11. Metrics and scorecard

### North-star metric

**Delivered, usable, referral-eligible campaign packages.**

A commitment or paid order that becomes late, poorly matched, or unusable is not a marketing success. While Gate B is closed, report customer commitments separately and do not imply that they are purchases.

A **customer commitment** is a dated, affirmative agreement from a qualified prospect to proceed once the currently closed payment/fulfillment gate is available, with campaign, audience, action, and timing recorded. It is not a sale, order, revenue, or payment.

### Weekly leading indicators

- qualified prospects researched;
- personalized first contacts;
- follow-ups;
- warm introduction asks;
- partner conversations;
- assigned-operator posts;
- sample-page visits;
- brief-page views, brief starts, and brief submissions;
- positive replies;
- fit checks;
- customer commitments;
- checkout sessions, only after Gate B opens;
- paid orders, only after Gate B opens;
- unknown attribution;
- activities logged within 24 hours and total activities;
- MailerLite active-subscriber and monthly-send usage;
- approval breaches.

### Quality indicators

- on-time delivery rate;
- clarification required before work starts;
- package usage confirmed;
- satisfied customers asked for referrals or permissioned testimonials;
- referrals received;
- repeat campaign intent;
- spam or inappropriate-message complaints;
- hard bounces and opt-outs;
- refunds or disputes;
- wrong-fit inquiries.

### Core rates

```text
Positive reply rate = positive replies ÷ first contacts
Fit rate            = qualified fit checks ÷ positive replies
Purchase rate       = paid orders ÷ qualified fit checks
Commitment rate     = customer commitments ÷ qualified fit checks
Sample-to-survey    = survey visits ÷ sample visits
Survey-to-payment   = future payment sessions ÷ survey starts (Gate B only)
Payment completion  = paid orders ÷ future payment sessions (Gate B only)
Referral rate       = customers making an introduction ÷ customers asked
```

Calculate these only for matured cohorts and leave a rate blank when its denominator is zero; never report a zero-denominator rate as 0%. Field definitions and cohort windows are authoritative in `docs/marketing/measurement-data-dictionary.md`.

## 13. Risks and controls

### Low price creates quality skepticism

**Control:** lead with visible scope, coordinated sample output, fact discipline, and human review. Do not compete on “cheap.”

### Brand-name drift remains visible

The GitHub/Vercel history still contains CampaignKit and CauseBrief naming.

**Control:** customer-facing links, profiles, email signatures, metadata, and outreach must consistently say **Snickerdoodle by Racoben**. Repository history should not appear in marketing copy.

### No customer proof at launch

**Control:** use clearly labeled fictional samples. Never pass them off as client work. Earn the first testimonial through delivery quality.

### Cold outreach looks like spam

**Control:** manual research, low volume, real trigger, correct person, one relevant sample, truthful subject, full business identity, clear opt-out, and immediate suppression after opt-out.

Commercial email must follow the FTC's CAN-SPAM requirements, including accurate headers and subjects, identification, a valid postal address, and an effective opt-out. The opt-out mechanism must remain able to receive requests for at least 30 days after each message, every request must be honored within 10 business days, and an opted-out address may not later be sold, transferred, or shared except with a provider used solely to comply with the law. Suppression is indefinite unless the person later gives documented affirmative consent. Gmail also requires authentication for all senders and imposes additional controls on bulk senders. Snickerdoodle should authenticate the sending domain with SPF and DKIM, maintain DMARC, and avoid turning assigned-operator outreach into bulk email.

### Marketing outruns operations

**Control:** use the capacity brake and do not promise a start time before complete intake. Reconcile and fulfill paid orders only after Gate B opens.

### Social effort produces attention but no buyers

**Control:** after 30 days, keep only channels that contribute qualified replies, sample visits, survey starts, partner introductions, or orders. Likes alone do not justify time.

### Unclear attribution

**Control:** use consistent source labels in the tracker and tagged links where practical:

```text
?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=operator_launch
?utm_source=direct_outreach&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=nonprofit_trigger
?utm_source=partner&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=first_commitment
```

Also ask every buyer, “How did you first hear about Snickerdoodle?”
